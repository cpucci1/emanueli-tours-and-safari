My story

From the shadow of Kilimanjaro to the Serengeti plains

I grew up in the Arusha region, in the shadow of Kilimanjaro, where safari trucks rolling through town toward the parks were simply part of everyday life. I started in tourism young — first as a porter and camp hand, then training as a driver-guide, learning to track game, read the weather over the crater rim, and keep a vehicle running on roads that don't forgive mistakes.

After years driving for larger operators, I set out on my own so that guests would get exactly what first brought me into this work: a guide who is genuinely present for the whole journey, not a name on a itinerary. Today I run Emanueli Tours and Safari as a small, independent outfit — my own Land Cruiser, my own bookings, my own standards.

I speak English and French fluently alongside my native Swahili, so whether your group is from Paris, Montréal, London or Brussels, you'll never need a translator between us.

How I work

The principles behind every safari

Respect for wildlife

Responsible distances, no off-track chasing, quiet observation over noisy pursuit.

Small groups only

One vehicle, up to 6 guests. No convoys, no crowding at sightings.

Punctual & prepared

Serviced vehicle, planned routes, and buffer time built into every day.

Community first

Fair wages, local suppliers, and park fees that fund conservation directly.
