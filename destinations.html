Five landscapes, one northern circuit

Every Emanueli safari moves through some combination of these five places. Here's what makes each one worth the drive.

Serengeti Ngorongoro Crater Tarangire Lake Manyara Kogatende
Serengeti
UNESCO World Heritage Site

Serengeti

The Serengeti's name comes from the Maasai word for "endless plains," and standing on that horizon is what most people picture when they imagine Africa. It's home to over three million grazing animals and the highest density of predators on the continent.

Whichever month you travel, some part of the Great Migration is usually within reach — calving on the southern plains, river drama in the north near Kogatende, or simply extraordinary year-round resident game in the central Seronera area.

Best time: Year-round Known for: Big cats, the migration, hot air balloons
UNESCO World Heritage Site

Ngorongoro Crater

The world's largest intact volcanic caldera drops 600 metres from the rim to a crater floor packed with an estimated 25,000 large animals — a natural enclosure so dense with game that a full day's drive rarely feels long enough.

It's one of the last strongholds for critically endangered black rhino in Tanzania, and one of the very few places where you can realistically see all of the "Big Five" in a single descent. Maasai communities still graze cattle along the crater rim above.

Best time: Year-round, cooler on the rim Known for: Black rhino, wildlife density, Maasai culture

Tarangire
Elephant country

Tarangire

Tarangire holds Tanzania's largest elephant concentration outside the Serengeti ecosystem — herds of 200 or more gather along the Tarangire River in the dry season, moving between ancient baobab trees that can be thousands of years old.

It's quieter than the Serengeti and Ngorongoro, which makes it a favourite first or last stop: superb birding (over 550 species recorded), occasional tree-climbing lions, and some of the most photogenic landscapes in northern Tanzania.

Best time: June – October (dry season) Known for: Elephants, baobabs, birdlife
Rift Valley lake

Lake Manyara

A dramatic wall of the Great Rift Valley escarpment towers over a soda lake that, in the right season, turns pink with thousands of flamingos. In between sits a dense groundwater forest — one of the few places in Africa where lions are known to climb trees.

Because the whole park is compact, it's easy to combine forest, lake shore and open grassland in a single half-day drive — and it's one of the few parks where guided walking safaris and canoe trips are possible.

Best time: November – May for flamingos Known for: Tree-climbing lions, flamingos, birding
Lake Manyara
Kogatende
Northern Serengeti

Kogatende

Kogatende is the gateway to the Mara River, where the Great Migration's most dramatic scenes play out between roughly June and October: thousands of wildebeest and zebra plunging across crocodile-filled water in a matter of minutes.

It's remote — a full day's drive from Arusha — and far less visited than the southern Serengeti, which means river-crossing viewpoints without the crowds. I usually build Kogatende into longer itineraries so the distance is worth every hour on the road.

Best time: June – October (river crossings) Known for: Mara River crossings, remote game viewing
