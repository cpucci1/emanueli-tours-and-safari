Planning your trip

Northern Tanzania is a year-round destination. June to October (dry season) is best for Tarangire's elephant concentrations and Mara River crossings near Kogatende. December to March covers the migration's calving season on the southern Serengeti plains. The "green season" (April–May) is quieter, cheaper, and still excellent for general game viewing.

On safari

Up to 6 guests per Land Cruiser, and I keep it smaller whenever possible so everyone gets a window seat and roof-hatch access. Private, exclusive-use vehicles are always available on request.
Lodge and tented-camp safaris put you in a fixed room or luxury tent with en-suite bathrooms and full service. Camping safaris use public campsites with shared facilities and mobile equipment — more adventurous, and significantly cheaper. Both use the exact same game drives and guiding.
No special fitness is required for game drives — you spend the day seated or standing through the roof hatch. Optional add-ons like a Kilimanjaro day hike or walking safaris involve more effort, and I'll always describe what to expect beforehand.
Neutral-coloured clothing (avoid bright white and dark blue, which attract tsetse flies), a warm layer for early crater mornings, a hat, sunglasses, sunscreen, binoculars, and a camera with a spare battery — charging is available in the vehicle. I'll send a full packing list once your trip is confirmed.

Health & safety

Most nationalities need a visa, available on arrival or online in advance via the official Tanzania e-Visa portal. Requirements change from time to time, so please confirm with your embassy or the official government site before you travel.
A yellow fever certificate is required if you're arriving from a country with risk of transmission. Malaria prophylaxis is strongly recommended for this region. Please consult a travel health clinic or your doctor a few weeks before departure for personalized advice.
Northern Tanzania's safari circuit is visited by hundreds of thousands of travelers every year and is generally very safe. As with anywhere, use normal common-sense precautions in towns, and inside the parks I follow strict wildlife-distance and vehicle-safety protocols at all times.
The vehicle carries a first-aid kit and satellite communication for areas without mobile signal, and I'm in regular contact with park rangers and lodge staff. This is exactly why travel insurance with medical evacuation cover is a non-negotiable part of any booking.

Money & booking

Message me your dates and group size, I'll confirm availability and send a written quote. A deposit (typically 30%) secures park permits and lodge bookings, with the balance due before your safari begins. Payment details are shared once your itinerary is confirmed.
US dollars (post-2009 bills, no marks or tears) are widely accepted for park-related payments; Tanzanian shillings are useful for small purchases. Tipping is optional but appreciated — a common guideline is around $15–20 per day for your guide, and similar for camp/lodge staff, adjusted to your experience.
